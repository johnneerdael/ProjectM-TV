ProjectM TV is a music visualizer for Android TV. It runs projectM 4.1.7, the open-source reimplementation of Winamp's MilkDrop, with 9,606 presets from Jason Fletcher's "Cream of the Crop" collection.

It visualizes the music another app plays on the TV, such as SoundCloud. It is not a music player itself: start your music first, then open ProjectM TV.

<b>What it does</b>
<ul>
<li>Shows 9,606 presets in shuffled order, changing every 30 seconds by default or with Left/Right on the remote, with smooth blends between them</li>
<li>Shows the cover, artist and title of the playing track in the upper left for as long as it plays; Settings › Track display shows them for 10, 20, 30 or 60 seconds per track instead, in a small lower-left pill, or not at all (needs notification access, under Apps › Special app access in the TV's settings)</li>
<li>Adapts the render resolution to hold the frame rate (30 fps by default), and caps it by the TV's memory so the music app is not closed</li>
<li>Renders with little memory traffic per frame, so TVs with a tile-based GPU (Mali, Adreno, PowerVR) reach much higher resolutions</li>
<li>Finds the audio session of the playing music app by itself, usually within a second or two of launch, whatever the TV's audio output (also Dolby or passthrough)</li>
<li>No freezes at preset switches: upcoming presets are prepared in the background</li>
<li>Replaces presets that stay black while music plays</li>
<li>Optional auto-update from the GitHub releases (off by default; the only network access). Not used when installed from F-Droid</li>
<li>Settings panel with transition style, detail, frame rate and live diagnostics</li>
</ul>

<b>Limits</b>
<ul>
<li>Based on testing, at least 2 GB of memory is highly recommended</li>
<li>Needs Android 5.0+ with OpenGL ES 3.0 and the permission to record audio (to receive the music, not the microphone)</li>
<li>Only Spotify, SoundCloud, SmartTube and Milkbeat have been verified as music apps. Track covers have only been verified with Spotify and Milkbeat</li>
<li>Audio that reaches the TV already encoded (e.g. Dolby bitstreams from a video app) cannot be visualized</li>
</ul>
